# Ping pong balls to hex: the lab's predictions BEFORE the real rig (21 Nov 2026, ~07:37 UK)

Mike's dream (21 Nov 2026): three ping pong balls, a small hole in each, hot air from above, the shells go pliable and the
balls go hex at the three-way point; in the dream it also worked with bean-bag poly beads. Built in the Delta lab
(lang/lab/src/pingpong-src.delta, d-math f3b3c51 + 93ae556) the same morning; the real rig (Mike's 2009 set-up, a new Henry
vacuum under the tray, his Skil 8007 MA heat gun with its 20 mm reduction nozzle) has NOT been run yet. These are written
and timestamped first so the real test can confirm or refute them honestly.

What is assumed (not proven): the gap's pull flattens the soft walls (the rate: softness x pull / 1,000,000 a second); the
plastic is pliable from 90 C, fully soft at 120 C, melting past 125 C; touching soft balls fuse where they touch. These are
rough guides. The gas rule, orifice flow, heat transfer and the fan's pull/flow trade-off are standard rules.

Predictions (3 x 40 mm balls, balls held near 118-120 C):
1. Without the Henry and with a 0.3 mm hole in each ball, they do NOT go hex: air leaves while heating and comes back faster
   than the balls cool (the most suck in the lab: 18 Pa).
2. Sealed balls (no hole) swell while hot (lab: up to ~28 kPa inside) and press flat where they touch.
3. With the Henry pulling under the tray, they go hex at the three-way point; the outer sides stay round. The stronger the
   pull, the faster (lab: Henry full ~80 s, half ~110 s, low ~190 s at ~118 C).
4. With the holes facing DOWN to the Henry, the tops cave in before the touching places fuse (more at higher pull).
5. The best warmth is ~118-120 C (Mike guessed 120): below ~100 C nothing changes; past 125 C they sag and drip.
6. On the Skil's top fan (500 l/min) its air comes out no hotter than ~220 C whatever the display is set to (2000 W limit).

## First real check of a lab rule (21 Nov 2026, 08:10 UK)
Mike's memory of his cold-air rigs (2009 era): no hex rafts. The funnel model with ball-on-ball grip (0.3) jams at 2 degrees
(matches); without grip it rafted ~60% hex (did not match): the grip is kept. Cold balls jam; hex needs heat + flow.
