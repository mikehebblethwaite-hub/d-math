The calculations

Every rule and every number behind the Hebble lattice results, first in D (as the lab runs it: whole numbers that only move, no zero, no minus, no π), then translated into the old maths, so anyone can check it either way. The code is open: every run can be repeated.

D numerals count in thirteens with digits 1–9, A–D and no zero (how to read them). In the tables, D is what the run prints and old is the same number written the old way.

1. Mass from just light

Code: lang/lab/runs/src/lightmass-src.delta. Run: delta run lang/lab/runs/lightmass.delta 3 79 1B.

The rules, in D

  • The sheet. A hexagon of cells, 6C cells from the middle to each side. Each cell holds how far it is lifted, kept above a rest level (nothing ever goes below anything).
  • The wave. Each step, every cell is pulled toward its six neighbours: its speed moves by (the neighbours’ lifts together, less its own lift for each neighbour) ÷ 8; then its lift moves by its speed.
  • The light. Every other side of the sheet is moved by a weight on a spring. Each step the weight’s speed is pushed back toward the middle by its distance out × 79 ten-thousandths, then it moves by its speed. The swing is counted, not worked out: 4B steps from one peak to the next.
  • Soaking up. Cells within C of a side lose part of their speed each step, so nothing bounces back.
  • Brightness. Over the last four swings, each cell adds up how far it is lifted, squared.
  • MAGIC (put in): mass forms where the light is brightest: a bright point is a cell brighter than every cell within two steps of it, and at least 1B hundredths as bright as the brightest.
  • Holes: a cell darker than every cell within two steps, and less than a twentieth as bright as the brightest.
  • Spin: round a ring of C cells two out from a hole, the step at which each cell peaks is read in turn; if the peak comes round once one way, or once the other, the hole spins that way.

The same rules in the old maths

  • The wave is the discrete wave equation on a triangular lattice: vc ← vc + ⅛ Σn(hn − hc), hc ← hc + vc, which is ∂²h/∂t² = c²∇²h with c = √(3/16) = 0.433 cells a step.
  • The spring is the harmonic oscillator (symplectic Euler) with k = 0.01: its period is 2π / arccos(1 − k/2) = 62.8 steps.
  • Wavelength λ = c × period = 27.2 cells. Three equal waves 120° apart make bright points six round one, 2λ/3 = 18.1 cells apart. The old maths calls the dark points phase singularities (optical vortices), with charge +1 or −1.

The step from bright light to mass is the one rule put in; that is the step still to be shown.

3. The blind wall test

The wall rule written blind by a helper who was never told the dream or any pattern (files and their stamps: proofs/blind-wall-2026-11-21/). Its own words, in the old maths:

  • Material: celluloid (2.7 g in a 40 mm ball with a 0.40 mm shell gives 1.34 g/cm³, as celluloid). Stiff, 2 GPa, up to 70 C; softening to 20 MPa by 110 C; flows from 140 C.
  • Force across the wall: press + pull × πRh. Where the wall settles: a² = F / (k/2R + πΔpair), with the shell’s stiffness k = 4Eh² / (R√(3(1−ν²))) and the heated air inside pushing back Δpair = 101325 (T − 293.15)/293.15.
  • It creeps there at a rate set by warmth; centres sit closer as the flat disc grows, each shell keeping its area.
  • Sealed: the disc reaches 1.9 mm (centres 0.17 mm closer). With a hole, or split at the seam (it splits near 124 C): about a third of the way flat (a 6 mm disc).

The test: balls scattered, never set in hex; hex judged by numbers (six-fold order round each ball). The pass mark, stamped before any run: mean six-fold order 0.7 or more, and 0.2 above both its start and the cold control. The result goes here when the runs finish, pass or fail.
